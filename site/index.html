Method and sources

  • Storage data: GIE AGSI+ (Gas Infrastructure Europe), country and EU totals through the AGSI+ API, refreshed daily. Each gas day shows the stock at the end of that day. AGSI+ publishes at 19:30 CET with a second run at 23:00 for late reporters; each update re-reads the last 60 days, because operators correct recent days. Days marked e are still estimated by the operator (status E), the rest are confirmed (C). Field definitions: AGSI+ data definitions.
  • Fill %: gas in storage as a share of working gas volume (AGSI+ “full”). TWh is gas in storage; capacity is the working gas volume.
  • 5-year average and range: the same calendar day in the five previous years (29 February uses 28 February), so 2026 is compared with 2021–2025 and a day in 2021 with 2016–2020. The range is the lowest and highest of those five. Shown only when at least three of the five years have data.
  • Net flow: injection minus withdrawal, GWh per day; positive means the country is filling. Long periods are shown as weekly or monthly averages.
  • % of annual consumption: AGSI+ “consumption full”, gas in storage against the country's reference annual consumption. ≈ days of consumption = that share × 365, i.e. days of average demand (winter demand is higher).
  • Filling target: under Regulation (EU) 2025/1733, EU countries with storage must reach 90% at any point between 1 October and 1 December, with room to deviate in difficult market conditions; the rules run until the end of 2027. The chart draws that line and window for EU countries and the EU total, not for the UK. Earlier years had a fixed date under Regulation (EU) 2022/1032: 80% by 1 November 2022, 90% by 1 November in 2023–2025.
  • LNG: GIE ALSI, LNG terminals by country: send-out (regasified gas sent into the grid, GWh/d) and LNG in tanks against the declared maximum inventory. Shown as 7-day averages, with the 5-year range of the same 7-day window.
  • Winter outlook: starts from the chosen day's fill level and adds, day by day, the change in storage of the same calendar days in each past year (gas in storage change divided by that year's working gas volume, so capacity changes don't distort it), kept between 0% and 100%. Each past year gives one path; the chart shows them all, their median and range. From April to September it becomes a refill outlook to 1 December. It is a what-if on past patterns, not a forecast: it doesn't know this winter's weather, prices or LNG supply, and a nearly empty store can't withdraw as fast as a full one.
  • Injection & withdrawal: injection and withdrawal are AGSI+ daily flows (GWh/d); net flow is injection minus withdrawal. Days left at this pace divides gas in storage (or the gap to 30% or 90%) by the last 7 days' average net flow, so it assumes that pace holds. Cumulative since the season started is the change in gas in storage since the end of 30 September (withdrawal season) or 31 March (injection season), in TWh. A season starts on the first day of a 14-day run in which the centred 7-day average net flow keeps the same sign, searched from the winter low (injection) or the autumn peak (withdrawal), so a short mild spell mid-winter doesn't count. Season totals add up daily injection (April–September) or withdrawal (October–March), so they are gross flows.
  • Winter so far: compares the change in gas in storage since the chosen start (end of 30 September, end of 31 October, or the day before the country's withdrawal season began) with the same calendar days in each past winter since 2016, as a share of each year's capacity; rank 1 is the biggest drop. Pace vs normal compares the average net flow over those days with the five previous winters. Tracking like starts the winter outlook from the chosen start and names the past winters whose paths stayed closest to what actually happened (smallest average gap). Milestones come from the outlook run from the chosen day, plus a straight-line projection at the last 7 days' pace. Germany's 30% on 1 February is the national rule for winter 2026/27 (Gasspeicherfüllstandsverordnung, in force until 31 March 2027).
  • Demand: Eurostat nrg_cb_gasm, monthly inland consumption of natural gas (code IC_CAL_MG, TJ on a gross calorific value basis, shown in TWh), published about two to three months late; recent months are often provisional. Share of a year's demand is gas in storage divided by demand in the 12 complete months before that day (or the latest 12 Eurostat has). The AGSI reference version uses the yearly consumption figure that AGSI+ itself publishes for each country (the basis of the EU rule's alternative target of 35% of annual consumption); it is a reported reference, not measured demand. Weeks of winter demand divides gas in storage by the average daily demand of the latest Dec–Feb before that day; it is not how long storage lasts, because production, pipeline imports and LNG keep flowing. Storage's share of winter demand is a month's net withdrawal (AGSI+) divided by that month's demand.
  • LNG terminals: GIE ALSI data for every LNG terminal in the EU since 2021, as ALSI lists them. The EU view adds them up by country (the seven largest by capacity, the rest as "Other"); its total can differ slightly from ALSI's own EU figure when an operator reports late. When a terminal changes operator, ALSI lists it twice; the charts join the two into one terminal. Use of capacity is the 7-day average send-out divided by the terminal's declared total reference send-out (DTRS), the capacity the operator declares. Spare is capacity minus send-out: how much more a country could regasify if cargoes arrived; it says nothing about whether cargoes are available. Tank level is LNG in tanks against the declared maximum inventory (DTMI). A terminal counts as idle when its 7-day send-out is below 5% of capacity. Terminals with no data in the last 30 days are left out.
  • What stands out: automatic notes for the chosen day: records for the calendar day across all years in the data, streaks outside the 5-year range, the fastest 7-day changes for that week of the year, the filling target, the season's peak, TTF and LNG send-out.
  • TTF: TTF spot, EUR/MWh.
  • Map: borders from entsoe-py (MIT) and Natural Earth (public domain). Grey countries aren't tracked yet.
